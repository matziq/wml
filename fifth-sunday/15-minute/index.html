Fifth Sunday · Missionary work

You Already Know How

One 15-minute lesson for the whole class — not a talk with questions bolted on. The class discovers the pattern in the scriptures, sorts real sentences out loud, finds their missionary style, moves to a corner, practices on a real person, and writes one commitment before they stand up. The leader talks for about four minutes total. Everything else is the class.

The one rule for this lesson: every time you finish a sentence, hand the room back. If you catch yourself explaining a beat that the class is already doing, stop mid-sentence and let them work. Silence in this lesson is not a problem to solve — it is people thinking of a name.

The 15 minutes

Say the bold part. Do the boxed part. Then get out of the way.

Beat by beat
0:00

Hands up — then turn and talk

Class works
Say

“Hands up if you have ever felt a nudge to invite someone to something — church, an activity, dinner, anything — and then talked yourself out of it before you said a word.”

“Look around. That is almost every hand in the room, and mine is up too.”

Activity · 40 seconds · pairs

“Turn to the person next to you. Twenty seconds each. Tell them the invitation you almost made and the reason you gave yourself for not making it. Go.”

Let the room get loud. At twenty seconds say “switch.” At forty seconds bring them back with one sentence:

Bring them back with“Everybody just said a version of the same reason: I’m not the missionary type. Hold that thought — we are going to take it apart with two verses.”
Do

Raise your own hand first and leave it up while you scan the room. Do not call on anyone and do not collect answers — the pairs are the point. Starting with talking, not listening, sets the rule for the next fifteen minutes.

1:00

Scripture hunt in pairs

Class works
Activity · 90 seconds · same pairs

“Same pairs. Open to John chapter 1. One of you takes verses 40 to 42, the other takes verses 45 and 46. You have ninety seconds and three questions:”

  1. Who did your person go find — a stranger or somebody they already knew?
  2. How many words did they use?
  3. When the other person pushed back, what did they do about it?

Scripture text — for this beat John 1:40–42 (KJV) — Andrew brings his brother

40One of the two which heard John speak, and followed him, was Andrew, Simon Peter’s brother. 41He first findeth his own brother Simon, and saith unto him, We have found the Messias, which is, being interpreted, the Christ. 42And he brought him to Jesus. And when Jesus beheld him, he said, Thou art Simon the son of Jona: thou shalt be called Cephas, which is by interpretation, A stone.

John 1:45–46 (KJV) — “Come and see”

45Philip findeth Nathanael, and saith unto him, We have found him, of whom Moses in the law, and the prophets, did write, Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph. 46And Nathanael said unto him, Can there any good thing come out of Nazareth? Philip saith unto him, Come and see.

Doctrine and Covenants 4:3, 5 — desire qualifies you

3Therefore, if ye have desires to serve God ye are called to the work; 5And faith, hope, charity and love, with an eye single to the glory of God, qualify him for the work.

Do · harvest, do not lecture
  • Take exactly three answers from three different pairs. Do not add to them.
  • If nobody says it, ask: “Nathanael said ‘can any good thing come out of Nazareth.’ What did Philip say back?” Let them say “come and see.” Do not say it for them.
  • Then read Doctrine and Covenants 4:3 aloud and ask one question: “What is the qualification in that verse?” Wait for the room to say desire.
Say · 20 seconds, then move

“So: somebody they already knew, three words, and no argument. That is the whole New Testament pattern, and the qualification is desire. Nothing on that list is about being outgoing or knowing the scriptures better than you do.”

3:00

Call it out: Love, Share, or Invite?

Class works
Say · 20 seconds

“The Church boils this down to three words: Love. Share. Invite. I am going to read six real sentences. You call out which one it is. Out loud, all together, no hands.”

Activity · 70 seconds · whole room, out loud

Read each line, pause one beat, and let the room answer. Some lines will split the room — that is the good part, not a mistake.

  1. “I brought your family dinner because I heard this week was hard.”Love
  2. “That hymn honestly got me through a rough week.”Share
  3. “We’re serving at ten on Saturday — want to ride with me?”Invite
  4. “I prayed about it and I just felt calmer.”Share
  5. “I saved you a seat.”Love
  6. “Would you like to come with me — and no worries either way?”Invite
Ask after the last one“Line six had four extra words on the end. What were they, and why do they matter?” Someone will say “no worries either way.” Answer in one sentence: “That half is what makes the first half safe enough to say out loud.”
Land it in one sentence, then stop

“Here is the part we miss: you do not have to do all three, in order, equally well. The Lord put different people in this ward on purpose — so let’s find out which one is already yours.” (Reference: Alma 17:11 · Alma 37:6)

Scripture text — for this beat Alma 17:11 — show forth good examples

11And the Lord said unto them also: Go forth among the Lamanites, thy brethren, and establish my word; yet ye shall be patient in long-suffering and afflictions, that ye may show forth good examples unto them in me, and I will make an instrument of thee in my hands unto the salvation of many souls.

Alma 37:6 — small and simple things

6Now ye may suppose that this is foolishness in me; but behold I say unto you, that by small and simple things are great things brought to pass; and small means in many instances doth confound the wise.

4:30

Take the quiz

Class works
Say

“Phones out. Scan the code, or go to wardmission.org and tap ‘Personality Test.’ Eight questions, two minutes. Pick what feels most like you — not the answer you think you are supposed to pick. Nothing is collected and there are no wrong answers.”

Do
  • Hold up the QR poster and leave it up the whole time.
  • Pair anyone without a phone with a neighbor, or hand them a paper copy.
  • Give a 30-second warning. Do not fill this time with talking — let it be quiet.
  • Protect this beat. If the class is running long, cut from the scripture hunt instead.
6:30

Stand up and go to your corner

Class works
Say

“Your result is a group name: Spark, Bridge, Beacon, or Seed. Stand up, find your sign, and go stand with your people. Take a pen.”

The SparkYou make the first move.Philip: “Come and see.”
The BridgeYou connect people through trust.Andrew, who went and got his brother.
The BeaconYou create a safe place to belong.“Let your light so shine.”
The SeedYou take quiet, sincere steps.Alma’s seed: small, planted, given time.
Do

Say the four names slowly, point to each sign, then stop talking and let the room move. Once everyone has landed, say one line: “Look at how many of you are not in the corner you assumed you’d be in.”

7:00

Corner huddle

Class works
Activity · 3 minutes · four corners at once

Each corner has a printed card (below). One person reads, everybody works. Give the whole room the same three instructions before they start:

  1. Read the top of the card out loud — who you are, your superpower, your growth edge. Thirty seconds.
  2. Everyone picks one line from the six and says it to the group in their own words. Not the card’s words — yours. Fifteen seconds each.
  3. The group answers one question: “Who did that line just make you think of?” Nobody says the name out loud. Everybody writes it down.
Call this out at 8:30“Ninety seconds left — make sure every person in your corner has said a line out loud.”
Do

Walk the room. Do not teach. If a corner stalls, ask one question and leave: “Which of these six would you actually say out loud?” The corner cards are printed in the next section — one per corner.

10:00

Cross the room

Class works
Activity · 90 seconds · mixed pairs

“Leave your corner. Find one person from a different corner — look at the signs, not at your friends. Thirty seconds each:”

  1. Say your style out loud and the one line you claimed.
  2. Ask them: “What would you do that I would never think of?”
Bring them back with“A ward of all Sparks would wear people out. A ward of all Seeds would never quite say it out loud. And a Beacon is the reason a Spark’s invitation works at all — because the friend walks in and somebody saved them a seat. You just met the person who covers your blind spot. That is not an accident, and that is why we need all four.”
11:30

Write it, then say it

Class works
Say

“Turn your card over. Three things, and be specific enough that you could not talk yourself out of it later: one name, one step, one day. Not ‘sometime.’ A day.”

Activity · 90 seconds · write, then pairs
  1. Sixty seconds of silence. Everybody writes. Do not talk over this — not even to encourage them.
  2. Thirty seconds: “Turn to the person beside you and read them your step and your day. Keep the name to yourself if you want. Saying it out loud is what makes it real.”
Seeds get one extra instruction“If you are a Seed, do not write the idea — write the exact words you are going to send. Then all you have to be is brave enough to press send.”
13:00

Popcorn out loud

Class works
Activity · 60 seconds · four voices

“Four people, no names, ten seconds each: just the step and the day. Who has one?”

Take one from each corner if you can — ask for “a Beacon” or “a Seed” by name if the same corner keeps answering. Say “thank you” after each one and nothing else. Do not comment, improve, or expand on anybody’s commitment.

14:00

Close and testify

Leader
Say · sixty seconds, then stop

“Fifteen minutes ago almost every hand in this room went up for an invitation we never made. Right now every person here is holding a name and a day.”

“Nobody is asking you to convert anybody. You are not responsible for the answer — only for the offer. A kind invitation that gets turned down is not a failure. It is a completed assignment.”

“I believe the Lord already knows who is waiting on you. He is not asking you to become a different person by next Sunday. He is asking you to be who you already are — on purpose, toward one person, this week. That is missionary work.”

Do

Bear a short, plain testimony of the Savior, then close. Do not add a summary of the lesson — they just lived it.

Corner cards

Print one per corner. These run the 3-minute huddle without a leader.

Handout

The Spark

You make the first move. — John 1:46, “Come and see.”

Read this out loud

If you landed here, your instinct is to act on a good feeling before you have worked out the details. Half this ward is waiting for somebody to go first. That is you — not because you are fearless, but because you move before the fear finishes its sentence.

Superpower

Your enthusiasm lowers the barrier for everyone else.

Growth edge

Your risk is not fear — it is speed. Invite, then stop talking. Let the silence belong to them.

Claim one and say it your way
  1. The 10-second invitation. “I’m going Sunday. Want to sit with me?”
  2. Offer one clear next step. “We’re serving at 10. I can pick you up at 9:45.”
  3. Ask, then listen. “Would that interest you?” Then wait.
  4. Make ‘no’ safe. “No worries at all — I’m glad I asked.”
  5. Bring a partner. “Will you come invite the Johnsons with me?”
  6. Notice the quieter person. “Come join us. I’ll introduce you around.”

Write on the back: one name, one step, one day. Sparks work best with a deadline — put a date on it.

The Bridge

You connect people through trust. — John 1:41–42, Andrew brought his brother.

Read this out loud

You do not lead with an invitation — you lead with the person. You remember what somebody told you a month ago. You notice when a name stops coming up. When you finally do invite, it lands, because they already knew you meant it.

Superpower

Nobody feels like a project around you. Your invitations are personal, never promotional.

Growth edge

Do not wait for a friendship to feel perfect. Waiting can start to feel like the loving thing when it is really just the comfortable thing.

Claim one and say it your way
  1. Start with their world. “How has your family been doing lately?”
  2. Connect the invitation. “You mentioned wanting more community, and this came to mind.”
  3. Share a small why. “Church has helped me feel less alone this year.”
  4. Use ordinary moments. “Can I tell you something that helped me this week?”
  5. Follow up as a friend. “How did that appointment go?”
  6. Set a gentle deadline. “I’m going to text Maya before Friday.”

Write on the back: one name, one step, one day. The name is already in your head — it has been for a while. Write it anyway.

The Beacon

You create a safe place to belong. — Matthew 5:16, “Let your light so shine.”

Read this out loud

You show what you believe long before you say it. You spot the need and quietly handle it, save the seat, show up with a meal nobody asked for. People experience the gospel through you before they have words for it.

Superpower

Consistency. Anybody can be impressive once — you are believable week after week. When a Spark invites a friend, you are the reason that friend stays.

Growth edge

Do not make people guess why you serve. If you never say why, they will decide you are just a nice person and miss the point entirely.

Claim one and say it your way
  1. Name your motivation. “My faith teaches me that nobody should carry this alone.”
  2. Be the welcome plan. “I’ll wait outside and we can walk in together.”
  3. Support someone else’s invitation. “You invite them; I’ll make sure they feel at home.”
  4. Share what sustains you. “Prayer has been helping me take this one day at a time.”
  5. Serve side by side. “Would you help us pack these kits Saturday?”
  6. Let consistency work. “I was thinking about you — how can I help this week?”

Write on the back: one name, one act of service, one day — plus the sentence you will say about why you did it. Do not skip that last line.

The Seed

You take quiet, sincere steps. — Alma 37:6 · Alma 32:28

Read this out loud

Loud is not your language. You think before you speak and would rather say one true thing than five impressive ones. In missionary work that is not a weakness to overcome — it is very often the reason people believe you.

Superpower

People who put their guard up around confident personalities will open up to you. Some people are only ever going to be reached by somebody quiet.

Growth edge

Fear shrinks when the next step is specific and tiny. You are not responsible for the answer — only for the offer.

Claim one and say it your way
  1. Use a prepared sentence. “No pressure, but I’d love to have you come with me.”
  2. Choose the smallest brave step. “Would it be okay if I sent you something that helped me?”
  3. Invite by text. “I’m going to this Sunday and thought of you. Interested?”
  4. Borrow courage. “Could you sit with me while I text Alex?”
  5. Practice out loud. Say it three times before you use it.
  6. Count the attempt. “Thanks for hearing me. Our friendship matters either way.”

Write on the back: one name, one day, and the exact words you will send — not the idea, the actual words.

Corner captains: you are a timekeeper, not a teacher. Read the top block, then make sure every single person says one line out loud in their own words. If your corner has fewer than three people, merge with the nearest corner and run both cards at once — the mixed conversation is better anyway.

Before class

Setup, handouts, and the “we’re running long” plan.

Leader notes

Print and prepare

  • QR poster for the quiz, large enough to read from the back row.
  • Four corner signs: SPARK, BRIDGE, BEACON, SEED — hung high enough to see from a standing crowd.
  • Style cards — one identity card per person, blank on the back for the commitment.
  • One corner card printed per corner (the four cards above), handed to a corner captain.
  • Pens or pencils on every chair. Nobody can finish this lesson without one.
  • Paper backup copies of the quiz for anyone without a phone.
  • The six sort sentences in large type, or read straight from this page.

Room setup

  • Hang the four corner signs before anyone arrives — people will read them during the opening and start guessing.
  • Leave the aisles clear. Two beats require the whole class to stand and move.
  • Seat your four corner captains near their corner so they can get there fast.
  • Test the quiz on the building’s guest network beforehand.
  • Write John 1:40–42 · John 1:45–46 · D&C 4:3 on the board before class starts.

Seed — Alma 37:6

Seed — Alma 32:28

28Now, we will compare the word unto a seed. Now, if ye give place, that a seed may be planted in your heart, behold, if it be a true seed, or a good seed, if ye do not cast it out by your unbelief, that ye will resist the Spirit of the Lord, behold, it will begin to swell within your breasts; and when you feel these swelling motions, ye will begin to say within yourselves—It must needs be that this is a good seed, or that the word is good, for it beginneth to enlarge my soul; yea, it beginneth to enlighten my understanding, yea, it beginneth to be delicious to me.

If you are running long: protect the quiz, the corner huddle, and the writing beat — those three are the lesson. Cut the scripture hunt to sixty seconds by assigning only John 1:45–46, and drop the popcorn beat entirely. Never cut the writing.

If you are running short: extend the cross-the-room beat to three minutes and add a second question — “What is one thing that would make it easier for you to actually do your step this week?”

If the room will not talk: do not fill it. Restate the instruction in fewer words, then count to five in your head. The second silence always breaks.
